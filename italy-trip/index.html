        How phone data should work

        Choose per household after comparing the carrier’s full-trip cost with a Europe-region eSIM. The phone must be unlocked to use another provider’s eSIM.

        Carrier roamingEasiest; keeps normal calling/data. Price the entire 20-day trip, not one day.
        Europe eSIMUsually better value for data. Install before flying; examples include Airalo, Saily, and Holafly.
        Hybrid — practical defaultKeep the U.S. line active for calls/SMS, use the eSIM for cellular data, and disable U.S.-line data roaming.
        • Confirm the phone is unlocked, supports dual SIM/eSIM, and will not charge for passive international use.
        • Keep the U.S. number available if banks or airlines use SMS; use WhatsApp for the group and local businesses.
        • Save the eSIM activation instructions offline. Do not remove the eSIM until everyone is home.
        Install + sign in

        Essential apps

        • Google Maps: download Rome, Puglia, Crete, and Athens offline; save every lodging, airport, rental desk, hospital, and fixed booking.
        • Google Translate: download Italian and Greek; the camera mode helps with signs and menus.
        • Delta + each intra-Europe airline: sign in before travel and confirm Hudson is visible where relevant.

        • Bank/card apps: turn on transaction alerts and make sure both parents can freeze their own cards.

        Take screenshots/PDFs anyway. An installed app can still log out, hide a booking, or fail without data.

        Useful, not mandatory

        Apps for this route

        • FreeNow or itTaxi: useful for licensed city taxis; for nine people plus bags, pre-booked vans are still more dependable.
        • Flighty or FlightAware: optional shared flight-status layer; airline notifications remain primary.
        • TheFork: optional restaurant discovery/reservations, especially after lodging locations are fixed.
        • Rental-company apps/accounts: useful for smart check-in, contracts, damage photos, and roadside contacts.
        • Apple/Google Wallet: add cards and eligible boarding passes, but carry physical cards for rental deposits.

        Skip for now: Ferryhopper unless a ferry is added; Trenitalia/Italo unless a train leg is added; Rome2Rio can explore options but should not be the authority for a critical transfer.

        Money + movement

        Small habits that matter

        • When a card terminal offers dollars or euros, choose euros and let the card network perform the conversion.
        • Carry two physical cards on different networks, stored separately, plus modest euro cash for small businesses and contingencies.
        • Tipping is not a U.S.-style obligation; check for service/cover charges and round or add a small amount for good service.
        • Use licensed white taxis in Rome. FCO to central Rome is currently a fixed €55 per vehicle within the Aurelian Walls.
        • Keep bags zipped and in front in crowded stations/sights; place trackers in checked bags and baby-equipment bags if already owned.

        American traveler cheat sheet

        Things that are easy to miss

        Passport + border

        • Every traveler—including Hudson—needs a passport issued within the last 10 years and valid at least three months after leaving the EU. Check now; six months of remaining validity is the comfortable buffer.
        • Italy and Greece are both in Schengen. EES is operating, so the first external-border arrival may include a facial image/fingerprints and take longer than the old stamp-only process.
        • Carry proof of lodging and onward/return travel offline. ETIAS is not currently expected before this trip.

        Power + plugs

        • Italy and Greece use about 230V, not U.S. 120V. A plug adapter changes the prongs; it does not change voltage.
        • Phones/laptops usually say “100–240V” and need only an adapter. Check every label.
        • Do not plug a 120V-only hair tool, kettle, bottle device, or other heater into 230V. Buy/borrow a local appliance or use a truly dual-voltage model.
        • Bring compact Type C/F-compatible adapters; Italy can also use Type L outlets.

        Driving

        • Road signs and speeds are metric. Benzina means gasoline; gasolio means diesel—check the fuel door and contract before filling.
        • Avoid ZTL restricted city-center zones even if navigation suggests the route. Camera fines can follow later.
        • At Italian tolls, avoid Telepass-only lanes unless the rental explicitly includes it; keep the entry ticket and use a card/cash lane.
        • Automatics and large vehicles are less plentiful than in the U.S.; reserve the required class and transmission explicitly.

        Restaurants + shops

        • Coperto is a per-person cover charge, not a tip. Water and bread may be charged; ask for acqua naturale (still) or frizzante (sparkling).
        • Dinner often starts later than in the U.S. Aperitivo can bridge the family’s early hunger without forcing a formal meal.
        • Smaller southern businesses may close for an afternoon riposo; check same-day hours before a special drive.
        • Coffee standing at the bar can cost less than table service. The posted table price controls, so ask before sitting in landmark areas.

        Tickets + toilets

        • If a paper Italian regional train ticket is ever added, validate it at the station before boarding. Current digital regional tickets auto-validate for the selected departure.
        • For tap-in/tap-out systems, use the same physical card or device at both ends; each rider generally needs a separate payment method.
        • Carry a few €1/€2 coins: some public toilets, lockers, small parking areas, or beach facilities may require cash.
        • In Greece, follow restroom signs about toilet paper; older plumbing often uses a nearby bin instead of flushing paper.

        Baby + pharmacy language

        • A farmacia / φαρμακείο is the first stop for many minor needs; look for the green cross and check after-hours duty listings.
        • Do not assume a taxi, transfer, or rental includes an acceptable infant seat—get the arrangement in writing or install the family seat.
        • Elevators, A/C, washers, high chairs, and cribs are not automatic lodging amenities. Confirm each one that matters.
        • Use the carrier for cobbles, stairs, Matera, and the gap between aircraft and baggage-claim stroller return.
