The Plan, In One Breath

Everything on this page is built around two things that make our trip special (and specific): a one-year-old, and grandparents who'd rather stroll than sprint.

We're keeping it slow and warm: a home base or two rather than a city-a-day sprint. That single choice quietly solves three problems at once — it's gentle on Hudson's nap schedule, kind to less-mobile grandparents, and it keeps us out of the densest crowds during peak-season August. Cities and big sights still happen; we just do them in unhurried day-trips and come home to a familiar bed.

The north star: nobody should be exhausted, nobody should be rushed, and every day should have a "do nothing" escape hatch built in.

Three Ways To Do It

Same spirit, different shapes. Pick by how much moving-around feels good for the group. All start in Italy and finish in Greece.

Recommended

A · The Slow Homebase

Least moving, most rest. Best fit for a baby + grandparents.

  • Week 1 — Umbria/Tuscan countryside villa flat, calm
    One house, kitchen, pool. Day trips to hill towns at our own pace.
  • Week 2 — Crete (Chania/Rethymno) accessible island
    One base on Greece's most accessible island. Beach mornings, tavernas, ruins optional.
  • Two homes, two unpackings, the whole trip. That's it.
Balanced

B · A Taste of the Icons

One great city up front, then slow down.

  • Days 1–4 — Rome golf-cart tours
    The big sights, done seated where possible. Central, elevator hotel.
  • Days 5–9 — Tuscan/Umbrian base recover & rest
  • Days 10–16 — Crete or Corfu accessible island
  • More variety; more transit. Front-loads the busy part.
Ambitious

C · The Grand Tour

See the most; accept more packing/unpacking.

  • Rome (3) → Florence (3) busiest
  • Athens (2–3) — Acropolis, then out
  • One accessible island (5) the reward
  • Most memories per mile, most fatigue per day. Only if the group has strong energy.
Why islands matter: Greece has ~200+ inhabited islands but only a handful are truly friendly to strollers, wheels, and tired knees. We favor Crete, Corfu, or Syros and steer clear of the stair-mazes (Santorini, we love you, but not this trip).
See Option A as a day-by-day timeline
  • Day 1 — Fly to Rome (or nearest hub), private transfer to the countryside. Collapse.
  • Days 2–6 — Villa life. One low-key hill-town day-trip every other day (Orvieto, Assisi, Montepulciano). Midday rest for everyone.
  • Day 7 — Short flight to Crete. Settle into home base #2.
  • Days 8–13 — Beaches, Chania old town (flat harborfront), Rethymno (has accessibility ramps), tavernas. Palace of Knossos as an optional half-day.
  • Day 14 — Fly home from Crete (or via Athens).

The One Health Thing 👶

Short version for the family. (Chris has the full sourced write-up.)

Measles is circulating in Europe (and honestly, in the US too right now), so Hudson's MMR vaccine timing matters. The plan: get him his measles shot about a month before we fly so he's fully protected on arrival, not still building immunity. Every adult traveling is already fully vaccinated, so nobody in our group can pass it to him — the only thing left to manage is crowds, which is exactly why we're going slow and rural-first.

Getting Around 🚕

Italy is cobblestones and hills; Greece is uneven and sunny. We move gently.

  • Private transfers over trains-with-connections when we move as a full group with luggage + baby. Door to door beats hauling.
  • In cities: seated options — Rome even has golf-cart tours of the big sights. Rent a wheelchair/rollator locally (via a pharmacy) for long days, even just in case.
  • Taxis: use FreeNow or Bolt apps (Uber is weak in Italy/Greece).
  • Venice, if we go: vaporetto water buses dodge the bridge stairs.
  • Islands: we pick accessible ones on purpose (Crete/Corfu/Syros).

Where We Stay 🏡

One rule solves it: kitchen + a confirmed elevator.

  • Apartments/villas with a kitchen — nap space, bottle-warming, laundry, and family dinners in when going out is too much.
  • Confirm the elevator ("lift") explicitly — European "first floor" often means a flight of stairs with no lift.
  • Book accessible rooms EARLY — city hotels have only 1–2 and they sell out months ahead.
  • Request a crib/pack-'n-play in advance; pick places near a pharmacy and hospital.

Good To Know Before We Go 🗺️

  • Meals run late. Italian dinner is 8pm+, Greek even later; shops often close midday. We'll do early or apartment dinners for Hudson and the grandparents.
  • Tipping is modest — service is often included ("coperto"). No US-style 20%.
  • August is hot & peak. Out in the mornings/evenings, rest midday. Book Greek ferries ahead.
  • A few words go far: grazie / ευχαριστώ (thank you), per favore / παρακαλώ (please). Translate app covers the rest.
  • Pharmacies (farmacia / φαρμακείο) are excellent for minor stuff — often faster than a doctor.
